          What stands out

          How the scores work. U-Haul publishes ranked top-five lists, not move counts. Each listing is scored by rank: #1 earns 5 points down to 1 point for #5 (ties share the rank). A metro's score is the sum across every origin list it appears on. State lists exclude each origin's own state, as U-Haul reports them. Scores show breadth and rank of appeal, not the size of flows, so compare them relative to each other.

          Rent data. Apartment List Rent Estimates, Vacancy Index and Time on Market, August 2026, metro level; "Landlord's" and "Renter's" market labels are Apartment List's test (vacancy, days to lease and rent change versus the national benchmark). Redfin's rent tracker has not been updated since 2025 and its apartment data now comes from Zillow, so it is not shown separately. Rent figures differ between sources because each measures a different slice of the market.

                Federal revenue this year

                About this data

                Revenue is what the Treasury actually collected (receipts), net of tax refunds. The gap between spending and revenue is the deficit, which the Treasury covers by borrowing. April is the big month because that is when most people settle their prior-year income taxes; corporate estimated payments land in mid-April, June, September and December, which is why those months also run high. Treasury publishes each month's figures about eight business days after the month ends.

                Where the money comes from

                  About this data

                  Sources follow the Monthly Treasury Statement's receipt categories. Individual income taxes include what employers withhold from paychecks plus quarterly estimates and April payments, minus refunds. Payroll taxes are the Social Security and Medicare taxes split between workers and employers (plus self-employment tax) and unemployment insurance; they are set aside for those trust funds. Customs duties are tariffs collected at the border. Federal Reserve earnings are the profits the Fed sends to the Treasury, which shrank to almost nothing when its interest costs rose. Change is versus the same months of the prior fiscal year.

                  Federal spending this year

                  Running total, this fiscal year vs. last

                  About this data

                  Each point is total spending from October 1 through the end of that month. Treasury publishes each month's figures about eight business days after the month ends, so the latest month lags. Totals are net outlays: some programs, such as deposit insurance and certain loan programs, take in more than they pay out, which lowers the total.

                  Where the money goes

                    About this data

                    Categories are the budget's roughly 20 "functions," which group spending by purpose (defense, health, transportation) no matter which agency pays it. Agencies show who writes the checks: Treasury's total is mostly interest on the debt, and Health and Human Services includes Medicare and Medicaid. "Other" is everything outside the top 10, net of offsetting receipts such as federal employees' retirement contributions, which is why it can be small or even negative. Change is versus the same months of the prior fiscal year.
